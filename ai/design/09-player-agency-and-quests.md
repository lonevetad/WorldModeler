# Player agency and quests

## Purpose

Connect world consequences to actionable opportunities without prescribing a
single player response. The framework can expose roles and quests derived from
the world state while leaving the player's choice to the game.

## Opportunity model

An opportunity can describe:

- the situation and relevant world entities;
- one or more possible actions or roles;
- requirements, risks, rewards, and affected parties;
- the facts and rules that caused it to be suggested;
- expiration or invalidation conditions;
- whether the opportunity is known to the player character.

Examples include guarding a royal vault, investigating an assassination,
escorting a caravan, negotiating an alliance, joining a rebellion, or opposing
a newly emerged threat.

## Quests

A quest may be represented as a goal or plan over events and state conditions,
not only as a fixed sequence of steps. It can have alternative paths,
dependencies, deadlines, optional objectives, and failure or abandonment
conditions. Quest progress should be derived from authoritative events where
possible, with explicit handling for ambiguous or contested evidence.

## Preserving agency

- Generate multiple feasible responses when appropriate, including no action.
- Treat opportunities as suggestions, not guaranteed mission assignments.
- Do not assume the player knows facts that only the simulation knows.
- Allow player actions to create events that alter, invalidate, or redirect
  existing opportunities.
- Keep game-specific morality, reward, and narrative policies outside the
  generic opportunity model.

## Player intervention in world conundrums

Some world conflicts can be exposed as decisions for the Player, such as
choosing between competing claims or influencing a faction's response. Each
decision opportunity should state its alternatives, known consequences,
deadline behavior, and eligibility requirements. Eligibility may depend on
location, access, knowledge, role, reputation, or other game-specific
prerequisites.

The simulation should not assume that the Player will intervene. The Player
may deliberately pass, be occupied elsewhere, lack the prerequisites, or fail
to respond before a timeout. The interface should distinguish an explicit
decline from an unavailable or expired decision when that distinction matters
to the game.

When intervention does not resolve the conundrum, hand it to the automatic
decision policy described in [rules and reasoning](08-rules-and-reasoning.md).
The world should continue to evolve; the Player's absence is not an implicit
pause. The resulting event and its reasoning should be visible in world history
to the extent appropriate for the Player's knowledge.

## Open questions

- Are opportunities generated only for the player, or for all agents?
- How are opportunity overload and relevance ranking handled?
- Should quest plans be generated by rules, authored content, planning
  algorithms, or a combination?
- Which decisions can be delegated, and which should be reserved for direct
  Player input?
